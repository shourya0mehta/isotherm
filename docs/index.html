Isotherm forecasts the day’s high temperature and trades on it.

It runs around the clock on Kalshi’s daily temperature markets at 24 U.S. airports, built entirely on free NOAA and ECMWF data. Each forecast is blended with live market prices, and Isotherm only trades where the price sits far enough from its estimate.

A day in the market

Every airport gets a new market each day, split into 2 °F brackets. Isotherm re-forecasts at set times, keeps its quotes fresh in between, and holds every position until the official report settles it. Times are Eastern.

  1. 10 AM

    The day before, Kalshi lists the brackets for tomorrow’s high.

  2. 11 AM, 2 PM

    Early bets, where the forecast’s edge over the market is largest.

  3. 5 PM, 11 PM

    Night-before forecasts from the evening model runs.

  4. 5 AM to 5 PM

    On the day, forecasts refresh every three hours and live sensor readings rule out brackets the high has passed.

  5. 11:59 PM

    Trading closes.

  6. 7 AM

    The next morning, markets settle on the National Weather Service’s daily climate report, East Coast first.

A live dashboard, on any phone

A private dashboard shows the account, the money at risk against every limit, profit by day, and which cities and bet types are working. The trading server publishes encrypted numbers every minute and the page decrypts them on the device, so it stays live without a laptop running anywhere.

How it works

Four stages run on one small cloud server, every day, on their own.

  1. Free data, lined up in time

    NOAA’s National Blend of Models, model output statistics and the 3 km HRRR, ECMWF’s open data, live airport sensors, and the official daily climate reports. Every input is matched to the minute it became public, so training never sees anything the live system couldn’t have.

  2. A forecast of the exact number

    A LightGBM model predicts a probability for every whole degree the official report could show. The night before, it misses by 1.57 °F on average against 1.92 °F for the best single NOAA product, and it beats that product at all 24 airports.

  3. Blended with the market

    The market is sharper than any single forecast, so Isotherm doesn’t try to replace it. It pools market prices with its own forecast using weights refit every week, and leans against the market when traders anchor on the government’s headline number.

  4. Trades inside hard limits

    Orders go in only where the price sits past the blend by the fee plus a margin. There are caps on every bracket, city and the whole account, daily and drawdown stops, one shared budget for cities whose weather moves together, and orders that expire within minutes if the server goes quiet.

Tested before it touched money

Model changes had to beat the version before them on rules written down before the test, and the trading code ran on Kalshi’s demo exchange before a real dollar went in.

  • Walk-forward tests. Every model is scored only on days after the ones it learned from.
  • A blind holdout. October and November are set aside and scored once, on December 1, by frozen scripts.
  • A leakage audit. Inputs rebuilt from data cut off at each decision time came out identical.
  • Market replays. The real trading code, run on recorded order books with pessimistic fill rules.
  • A rival bot. On Kalshi’s demo exchange, a second account traded against Isotherm on purpose.
  • 622 automated tests, including a 40,000-cycle chaos test with injected API failures.
